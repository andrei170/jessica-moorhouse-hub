Foundation

Copy Strategy - Jessica Moorhouse

Foundation doc · Voice, brand pillars, headline bank, framing AG1 will write to.

Voice

Warm, conversational, peer-to-peer. Jessica's brand is built on making money feel less intimidating for Canadians who grew up not talking about it. The voice is direct without being cold, knowledgeable without being condescending, and personal without oversharing. QAFP credential (Nov 2025) adds a layer of earned authority that should be worn lightly, not lead with.

Use: clarity, practical, Canadian, real, specific, steps, system, progress, plan, honest, straightforward, earned, qualified, working through it Avoid: game-changer, revolutionary, wealth mindset, manifesting money, financial freedom (overused), hustle, grind, passive income (in a generic sense), abundance, quantum, secrets, hacks, unlock

Brand Pillars

  1. Money as a life skill, not a personality - The book title "Everything but Money" signals that money is the thing keeping people from living the life they actually want. Copy reflects that money mastery is a tool, not an identity.
  2. Canadian-specific - TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, the real estate conversation, the cost of living in major Canadian cities. Copy is not generic "build wealth" content. It is built for the Canadian millennial navigating Canadian realities.
  3. Earned authority - QAFP designation, years of podcast publishing, a bestselling book. Credibility is real. Copy references it matter-of-factly, not boastfully.
  4. Accessible without being basic - The audience is smart and time-pressured. Copy respects their intelligence and gives practical next steps, not just inspiration.

Headline Bank

For the Everything but Money Signature Programme landing page, hub callouts, and content:

  • The course that turns the book into your actual financial plan.
  • Built for Canadians who are tired of advice that doesn't fit their life.
  • The money system your bank won't give you and most podcasts won't build.
  • Six modules. QAFP-informed. Built by a Canadian for Canadians.
  • If you've read the book, this is what you do with it.
  • Finally understand your money. Then actually change it.
  • The programme for people who know what to do and still aren't doing it.
  • Debt, savings, income, investing. In that order. For real this time.

Framing for the Funnel

  • Top of funnel (quiz, content, IG hooks): emotion-led, not technical. "Why am I still stressed about money even though I earn good income?" beats "Here are three budgeting frameworks."
  • Mid funnel (programme landing, VSL preview): "Everything but Money" as the bridge. The book gave them the permission; the programme gives them the system.
  • High tier (1:1 coaching): qualified buyer framing. "You've done the programme. You know your numbers. Here is what a personal session adds."

Banned Phrases

Universal AG1 ban list, plus Jessica-specific: - No "financial freedom" (overused in Canadian money content, no longer meaningful) - No "build wealth" as a standalone claim without a specific mechanism - No "invest in yourself" (tired) - No generic American retirement references (401K, IRA, Roth) - use TFSA/RRSP/FHSA - No "manifest" or "abundance mindset" language - No dollar figures that assume US income benchmarks

Tone Calibration By Surface

Surface Register Length
IG hook Warm, specific, conversational - like a text from a money-smart friend 1-2 lines
Ad copy Emotion-led hook + QAFP credibility qualifier 2-3 lines
Email subject Curiosity or specific claim, never clickbait 5-8 words
VSL opening "I spent 10 years making money easier to talk about for Canadians." 1 sentence cold open
Landing hero What changes when you actually have a plan 1 promise + 1 mechanism
FAQ Warm and direct. Acknowledge the Canadian-specific concern. 2-3 sentences

Voice Test

Every paragraph passes if a 31-year-old Canadian who earns $75K, has $18K in credit card debt, and has been "meaning to sort out their TFSA for two years" reads it and feels seen, not sold to. If it could appear in a generic "make money online" course, rewrite it.
